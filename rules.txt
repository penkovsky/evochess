 The EvoChess Game
 =================

 == EvoChess rules

 0. === Chess-Based Rules
    - In general, the pieces move and the Pawns are promoted similarly to chess.
    - During the promotion, only the pieces of the same color are available.
    - The first long Pawn move is allowed and is counted as a single move.
    - En passant is defined as in chess.
    - Checks, mates, and stalemates are defined by the standard chess rules.

 1. === EvoChess Starting Position
    The game starts only with 8 Pawns and a King of each color
    on their standard chess positions.

 2. === The First Evolutionary Addendum aka "To Minor Piece Promotion"
    In addition to chess rules, Pawns may be promoted to minor pieces
    under the following conditions. After every N = 3 Pawn moves,
    the player gains the right to promote to a minor piece (a Knight or a Bishop)
    the last Pawn that moved.

 3. === The Second Evolutionary Addendum aka "To Rook Promotion"
    After every M = 3 moves made by a player's own minor pieces (Knights
    and Bishops), that player gains the right to promote to a Rook the last
    minor piece that moved. This fully mirrors the Pawn rule: just as the
    "To Minor Piece Promotion" right promotes the last Pawn that moved, the
    "To Rook Promotion" right promotes the last minor piece that moved.
    Captures play no part in the "To Rook Promotion" right.

 4. === Clarifications
    - The promotion rights, both for Pawns and minor pieces, are accumulated
      indefinitely.

      ==== Consider the white Pawns promotion example

           | Move # | Comment
      ======================================================================
      e4   | 1      | A Pawn moves.
      ..   |        |
      d4   | 3      | Another Pawn moves.
      ..   |        |
      d5   | 5      | The same Pawn moves again, and the "To Minor Piece Promotion"
           |        | right is obtained. No promotion is obligatory.
      ..   |        |
      d6   | 7      | A Pawn moves without promotion.
           |        | The player's "To Minor Piece Promotion" right from the
           |        | previous moves is preserved.
      ..   |        |
      b4=N | 9      | A Pawn moves and is promoted to a minor piece.
      ..   |        |
      Nd5  | 11     | The minor piece moves. No evolution.
      ..   |        |
      a4=B | 13     | The 6th Pawn move counts towards regaining the
           |        | "To Minor Piece Promotion" right. The Pawn gets promoted.

      Remark: The next N - 1 = 2 Pawn moves, Pawns have no evochess promotion
              rights unless the 8th rank was reached.

      Remark: The 8th rank Pawn promotion is obligatory as in standard chess
              and is also counted towards the Pawn moves.

    - A move by a minor piece counts towards "To Rook Promotion" whether or
      not it is a capture. Pawn moves (including Pawn captures) count towards
      "To Minor Piece Promotion" only, never towards "To Rook Promotion".

    - A Pawn move on which the Pawn evolves into a minor piece (via the
      "To Minor Piece Promotion" right) is counted as a Pawn move: it counts
      towards "To Minor Piece Promotion" and never towards "To Rook Promotion",
      because the piece that moved was a Pawn. The freshly created minor piece
      only begins counting towards "To Rook Promotion" on its own later moves.

    - En passant is unaffected by an evolution on the same move. If a Pawn
      makes its first long move and evolves into a minor piece at the end of
      that move, the en passant capture is still available to the opponent on
      the next move, exactly as if no evolution had taken place. The capturing
      Pawn moves to the skipped square and removes the evolved piece, minor
      piece or not. Rationale: the right to capture en passant is created by
      the Pawn move, and the piece that moved was a Pawn; an evolution resolves
      only at the end of that move and cannot retroactively undo it. Otherwise
      spending a promotion right would double as an en passant immunity.

    - A promotion is available only in the end of the player's move.

    - Both evolutionary promotions apply to the piece that just moved: the
      "To Minor Piece Promotion" right may only be spent on the Pawn that
      just moved, and the "To Rook Promotion" right may only be spent on the
      minor piece that just moved. Consequently a rook-promotion right can
      only be exercised on a turn on which a minor piece moves, just as a
      minor-promotion right can only be exercised on a turn on which a Pawn
      moves. Unused rights are preserved for a later such turn.

    - Only one piece at a time can be promoted even if several rights are
      accumulated (e.g. an unused Pawn-promotion right together with an unused
      minor-piece-promotion right).

    - If the 8th rank is reached by a Pawn, the Pawn promotion is obligatory.
      No other promotions are possible at that move.

    - Castling is not defined.

 5. === The Third Evolutionary Addendum aka "Rook Charges"
    A Rook is a one-shot power spike, not a permanent fixture. Every Rook
    carries 5 charges, starting from the moment it is created (whether by
    the "To Rook Promotion" right or by a standard 8th-rank Pawn promotion
    to Rook).

    - A charge is spent only when the Rook itself moves. Moves by other
      pieces do not affect a Rook's charges.

    - When a Rook move brings its charges to 0, the Rook immediately
      downgrades, on the same square and the same turn, into a minor piece
      (a Knight or a Bishop, the owner's choice). This downgrade is
      mandatory: a Rook move that would exhaust its last charge cannot be
      completed without it.

    - The downgraded minor piece begins a fresh "To Rook Promotion" counter
      at zero, like any other minor piece, but it can never again be
      promoted to a Rook: once downgraded, a piece is permanently locked
      out of the "To Rook Promotion" right, even if it later earns one.

    - Capturing a Rook is a normal capture: the Rook and its remaining
      charges are simply removed from the game. Capture never triggers a
      downgrade — downgrade only ever results from the Rook's own move
      exhausting its own charges.

    - Because a downgrade can remove the very piece giving check (or bring
      a new piece into a checking position), check, checkmate, and
      stalemate are judged only after the downgrade is resolved, not on
      the intermediate position immediately after the Rook's move.


September 2017, Bogdan Penkovsky

Revised July 2026: the "To Rook Promotion" right was changed from being earned
by non-Pawn captures (K = 2) to being earned by minor-piece moves (M = 3),
making it structurally symmetric with the Pawn rule and rewarding activity
rather than captures.

Revised July 2026: the "To Rook Promotion" right was further narrowed so that
it promotes only the last minor piece that moved (rather than any minor piece
on the board), completing the symmetry with the "To Minor Piece Promotion"
right, which promotes only the last Pawn that moved.

Revised July 2026: clarified that an evolution on a first long Pawn move does
not cancel the opponent's en passant capture, resolving an ambiguity between
"En passant is defined as in chess" (rule 0) and evolution changing the piece
on the arrival square.

Revised July 2026: added "Rook Charges" (rule 5), so that a Rook is a scarce,
temporary asset rather than a permanent fixture once earned. Charges are
spent per Rook move, not per turn, so developing other pieces costs a Rook
nothing. The permanent lock-out on re-promotion (rather than allowing a
downgraded piece to become a Rook again) keeps Rooks a burst of power to be
timed rather than a piece that cycles indefinitely.
